# WFD — What's for Dinner · Changelog

Versions are MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH:
- **PATCH** (1.1.x) — small tweaks and fixes.
- **MINOR** (1.x.0) — new features.
- **MAJOR** (x.0.0) — big milestones (e.g. the app-store launch, a redesign).

## 1.16.1 — Nov 25, 2026
- You can now email us at support@wfdmeals.app (listed on the Help and Privacy pages).

## 1.16.0 — Nov 25, 2026
- Flow chart, easier to read: steps keep their own wording ("Crisp the bacon", "Make the guacamole sauce") instead of losing the ingredient names, and recipe headings like "FOR THE TACOS" no longer get mixed in.
- Long recipes, or charts too wide for your screen, show step by step, top to bottom, with the ingredients each step brings in. Switch between **Chart** and **Step by step** any time.
- Ingredients no step mentions (salt, pepper…) are listed under the chart instead of leaving empty rows; setup steps ("Preheat the oven") sit above it.
- New colors that match the rest of the app.

## 1.15.2 — Nov 25, 2026
- WFD has its own address: **wfdmeals.app**. The old link forwards there; sign in once more on the new address.

## 1.15.1 — Nov 25, 2026
- Flow chart: catches tips that an imported recipe glued onto the next step, and keeps setup steps ("Heat oven to 350°F") readable on wide charts.

## 1.15.0 — Nov 25, 2026
- Prices refresh on their own every Monday for the stores you shop at, so the list totals stay current without you tapping anything. Prices you typed in, locked, or got from a recent receipt are never overwritten.
- Cook-day timers now start only for hands-off steps (bake, simmer, roast, marinate, rest, rise, steep, chill…), not for stirring, frying or searing.
- Flow chart: tips, swaps and serving notes ("you can use broth instead…") no longer show up as steps.

## 1.14.1 — Nov 24, 2026
- Flow chart: tidier column widths so the steps fit on screen.

## 1.14.0 — Nov 24, 2026
- Recipes as a flow chart: open any recipe and switch to 🔀 Flow chart to see ingredients down the left and each step as a block joining what it uses (setup like "Preheat the oven" across the top). Tap a step for its full text. Also on Prep's recipe cards, with amounts scaled to your batch.
- Project carts: "+ Add a recipe" opens the same picker as everywhere else — search your recipes (typos are fine) with photos and nutrition, find new ones, or paste a link.

## 1.13.0 — Nov 24, 2026
- Change how many of anything to buy: every item on the Shop list has − / + buttons. Your number sticks for this week ("reset" goes back to what the recipes need) and the trip and total update.

## 1.12.1 — Nov 24, 2026
- Fix: recipes saved from the recipe finder no longer ask for hundreds of packages (gram amounts were being counted as packages), and leftover unit words like "Tblsp" are cleaned off item names.
- Every item gets a price estimate now, even unusual ones (a typical price for its aisle when there's nothing closer).

## 1.12.0 — Nov 24, 2026
- Price estimates for everything: items nobody has priced yet get a typical price, adjusted for each store (cheaper at Costco, Walmart, Grocery Outlet and Trader Joe's; more at Safeway, Raley's and Whole Foods), marked "est." Receipts, prices you type and live lookups still win.
- Shopping amounts make sense: one jar of a spice or sauce is enough however many times you make the recipe, and imported amounts in grams no longer turn into "buy 100".
- Cleaner item names from new recipes ("Finely chopped Carrots" → "Carrots", "100g Minced Beef" → "Minced Beef"), and your existing items are tidied up once.
- Typos are understood: Find new searches "chiken tika masala" as "chicken tikka masala" (with a "search for what I typed" link), Saved recipes search forgives slips, and the Pantry box suggests the item you meant as you type.
- Shopping trips start from home and end at your last store. Want a round trip? Turn it on in Settings or with one tap on Shop.

## 1.11.2 — Nov 24, 2026
- Fix: the app could get stuck on "Loading…" (and the bottom bar stopped working) once a side dish like Steamed rice was saved. Sorry about that.
- Fix: tapping a text box on iPhone no longer zooms the screen in.

## 1.11.1 — Nov 24, 2026
- Privacy policy now covers barcode scanning (Open Food Facts) and shelf photos (Anthropic).

## 1.11.0 — Nov 24, 2026
- Pick for me gives real variety: it no longer gets stuck on the same two or three dishes. It looks for 1.5× as many fitting recipes as it needs (bringing in new ones from your cuisines, then from any cuisine), and mixes proteins and styles.
- Repick: tap ♡ on the meals you like and "Keep ♥, repick the rest", or "Repick all" for a fresh set of different options. Changing cuisines also gives new picks.
- Fairer diet checks: Diabetes-friendly now looks at carbs and added sugar per serving (a teaspoon of sugar or a squeeze of lemon no longer rules a dish out). Low-FODMAP suggests swaps for onion and garlic (green onion tops, garlic-infused oil) instead of rejecting the dish. Pregnancy-safe no longer flags sushi rice, and cooking wine is a note, not a no.
- Sides: Pick for me adds a side (rice, bread, potatoes, tortillas…) to light lunches and dinners, so meals are filling. Remove any side with ✕, or add one with a tap. Sides go on the shopping list and in Prep, and count toward nutrition.
- Ready for your own web address: change one setting and the app, invite links and install icon all move to it.

## 1.10.1 — Nov 24, 2026

## 1.10.0 — Nov 24, 2026

- Week at a glance: the Plan shows average calories, protein, carbs and fat per person per day for the meals you've placed, and how many meals miss your goals.
- Protein goal: alongside the calorie goal, set a minimum protein per meal. Pick for me only chooses (and finds) main meals that meet both.

## 1.6.0 — Nov 24, 2026

- Clearer diet and allergy checks: each recipe now says exactly which ingredient doesn't fit ("Not Low-FODMAP — garlic, onion"), and shows a ✓ for the diets it does fit. Look-alikes no longer trip the checks (garlic-infused oil, lactose-free milk, green beans, butternut squash, almond flour, a bunch of cilantro). Keto, low-carb, low-fat and high-protein are judged from the nutrition numbers instead of word lists.
