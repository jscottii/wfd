How to spend less on groceries without clipping coupons

Practical ways to lower the weekly bill that don't take extra time.

Coupons and deal-hunting can save money, but they take time most families don't have. These habits lower the grocery bill without any extra work once they become routine.

Shop from a plan, not from the aisles

The single biggest saver is walking in with a list built from your week's meals. Unplanned buying is where budgets leak: the extra snacks, the second jar of something you already had.

Know where things are cheapest

Prices for the same item can differ a lot between stores in the same town. Staples like rice, beans, oil, spices and canned tomatoes are often much cheaper at warehouse or discount stores, while produce may be cheaper elsewhere. Splitting a list between two stores can save real money, as long as the extra drive is worth it.

Count the drive

A second store saves money only if the savings beat the gas and the time. A good rule: only add a stop if it saves more than a few dollars per extra mile, and group stores that are near each other.

Buy for the whole week, not for one recipe

When two recipes use the same ingredient, a bigger package is usually cheaper per ounce. Plan meals that share ingredients so nothing goes to waste.

Use what you already have

Food you throw away is money spent twice. Check the fridge and pantry before planning, and build at least one meal around what needs using up.

Swap brands on the basics

Store brands of flour, sugar, canned goods, frozen vegetables and dairy are usually made to the same standards for less. Save the name brands for the few things where you notice a difference.
