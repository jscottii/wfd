How to plan a week of dinners in 15 minutes

A simple routine that turns "what's for dinner?" into a decision you make once a week.

Most of the stress of weeknight dinners isn't cooking. It's deciding, every single evening, while everyone is hungry. Planning once a week moves that decision to a calm moment and makes shopping faster too. Here's a routine that takes about fifteen minutes.

1. Look at the week first, not the recipes

Before picking a single meal, look at the calendar. Which nights are busy with practice, late meetings or plans out? Those nights need something that's already made or takes under 20 minutes. A calmer night can handle a recipe that takes longer. Write down how many dinners you actually need. It's often five, not seven.

2. Pick from a short list of favorites

Keep a running list of 15 to 20 meals your household already likes. Each week, choose most of your dinners from that list and add one new recipe at most. New recipes take longer to shop for and cook, so one is plenty.

3. Cook once, eat twice

Choose at least one meal that makes extra on purpose: a big pot of chili, a double pan of baked ziti, or extra rice for fried rice later in the week. Leftovers are a planned dinner, not an accident.

4. Share ingredients across meals

If Monday's fajitas need a bag of peppers and Thursday's fried rice needs vegetables too, buy once and use them up. Overlapping ingredients cut waste and cost. A bunch of cilantro or a head of cabbage can show up in two meals instead of wilting in the drawer.

5. Check what you already have

Before writing the list, look in the fridge, freezer and pantry. Anything that needs using soon should shape the plan. Half a bag of spinach becomes baked ziti; leftover rice becomes fried rice.

6. Write the list by store section

Group the list the way the store is laid out: produce, meat, dairy, pantry, frozen. You'll walk through once instead of zig-zagging back for the one thing you missed.
