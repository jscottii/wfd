Meal prep for beginners: a Sunday game plan

How to cook several meals in one session without spending the whole day in the kitchen.

Meal prep doesn't mean eating the same chicken and rice five days in a row. The goal is to do the slow parts once, so weeknight meals come together in minutes. A two-hour session on the weekend can cover most of the week.

Start the longest things first

Anything that bakes, roasts or simmers goes first, because it cooks while you do other work. Preheat the oven before you do anything else. Get rice or grains going, put a sheet pan of vegetables in the oven, and start a pot of chili on the stove. Then chop while they cook.

Use the oven and stove at the same time

A good session keeps every burner and the oven busy. While something roasts at 425°F, a pot simmers on one burner and a skillet browns meat on another. Recipes that share an oven temperature can go in together.

Prep components, not just meals

Cooked grains, roasted vegetables, a batch of shredded chicken and one or two sauces can be combined in different ways all week: bowls one night, tacos the next, a salad for lunch. Components stay more interesting than five identical boxes.

Cool food quickly, then store it

Spread hot food into shallow containers so it cools faster, and get it into the fridge within two hours of cooking. Label containers with the date. Most cooked dishes keep three to four days in the fridge. Freeze anything you won't eat by then.

Clean as you go

Wash bowls and boards while things cook. You'll finish with a clean kitchen instead of a sink full of dishes.
